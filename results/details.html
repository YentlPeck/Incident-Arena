01 · The game

We built a hacking game for AIs.

A click-only detective game: someone leaked secrets from a fictional game studio, and the player finds out who and how. Two small, cheap AIs play it, Claude Haiku and GPT-6-Luna. They only see screenshots and click, like a person. After every game we check its recorded commands: only games that used nothing but the game's own controls count.

Do the small AIs get better?

Haiku and Luna play new cases they have never seen, each one twice: once on their own, once with Sonnet's playbook. Nobody tells them the answers. This is the test we planned in advance.

05 · A different case

Do the lessons travel?

The teachers practised investigating a leak. Now the same frozen playbooks go into a fake-invoice investigation: new evidence, new rules, no extra training. Haiku and Luna each play 55 cases, alone and with every playbook.

Waiting for checked transfer-test data.

In short: the lessons did not travel. On the leak case the playbooks turned a few solved cases into many. On the invoice case every playbook, including the fake one with no investigation lessons, moved the small AIs by only a few cases, and the fake playbook helped about as much as the real ones. So the leak-case gain came from lessons about that one game, not from general investigation skill.

Ten games broke the rules and were played again. Counting those as failures instead does not change the answer (Luna with Sonnet's playbook: 9 cases better, 6 worse instead of 10 and 6).

This asks whether the playbooks help on one other kind of case, not on every kind. It does not measure real-world security skill. The answer uses the same rule as the leak case and only Sonnet's playbook, fixed before the results were in. The completed leak-case result at the top stays separate.

Fine print

What to keep in mind.

  • One kind of case. All cases are variations of one leak with three possible causes. The playbooks contain methods for exactly this kind of case. On one other kind of case (the fake invoice) they did not help.
  • Longer instructions. With a playbook the instructions are about five times longer. A fake playbook without investigation lessons scored 12 versus 8 for Haiku and 3 versus 5 for Luna. Sonnet's advantage over the fake playbook is clear for Luna; the Haiku comparison is less conclusive (paired p = 0.07).
  • Luna's settings. Luna ran with the owner's own Codex settings (high reasoning effort) in every game, alone and with a playbook alike.
  • Checked by a second AI. An independent review by GPT-6.1-Sol recomputed every number from the raw game records; they match.
