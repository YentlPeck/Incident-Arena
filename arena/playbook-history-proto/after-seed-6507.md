# Locked-Room Prototype Playbook

## Goal and framing
- Every case asks who carried the prototype out, how it left the locked room and the building, and which two records together prove it.
- Read the house rules first. They define what counts as proof, how records are filed, and quirks such as which reference on a request governs it.
- A correct filing needs the right person, the right way out, and an evidence pair the game accepts. A right guess with weak evidence still fails.
- Cases often accept two pairs. Test each candidate pair against what each record literally shows, not what it implies.

## Opening moves
- List every record type first: door or badge logs, bookings, calendars, chats, visitor logs, tour or escort schedules, corridor notes, inventory checks, exit or bag-check logs, pickup requests, carrier logs, work tickets.
- Fix the window between the prototype's last confirmed presence and first noticed absence.
- Pull the locked-room door log for that window. Each opening is a candidate moment.

## Linking records
- For each opening, ask who opened it, why (booking or task), and who else was near at that minute.
- A legitimate booking explains the opening, not the outcome. It clears the opener, never whoever followed them in.
- Match bookings to the exact minute. A booking by the same person at another time does not cover an entry outside that slot.
- A calendar slot or the person's own chat placing them elsewhere at an entry's minute is strong evidence of an unjustified entry.
- People without badge access (visitors, tours, contractors, delivery staff) can only enter through someone else's opening. Check tour routes and escort schedules for a group passing the room's corridor at an opening's minute.
- Tie each visitor to their group via the visitor log or escort list, so a group-level placement applies to them personally.
- A record placing someone at the doorway at the exact minute it opened is the strongest link.
- Follow the item to the building exit: badge-out, sign-out, end of escort, or a carrier handover. Match the handover time to the fetch time.

## Ways out and the pairs that prove them
- Tailgating: the door log row for that opening plus a record putting the suspect at that doorway then, such as a tour route, escort schedule, or corridor note. Each independent placement record can complete its own valid pair.
- Misused badge: an entry in the holder's name plus a record showing the holder was elsewhere.
- Insider without reason: their own entry in the window plus a record showing no booking or task covers it, such as a calendar or chat placing them elsewhere, or their only booking at another time.
- Carried out by a person: presence at the room plus a later exit record; bag-check gaps strengthen it.
- Fraudulent outside pickup: a legitimate fetch handed to a carrier, triggered by a request posing as a known partner and quoting a reference that does not authorise release. Pair the carrier pickup row (actual collection and quoted reference) with the ticket showing that reference is closed, old, or not for the prototype.
- When a request quotes several references, check which governs it (the first quoted has so far). A reference quoted only "also" is often a decoy, even if open.
- Confirm before filing: one record must show the room entry or the item leaving, the other must show why that was illegitimate and tie it to the person or carrier.

## Tempting pairs that do not prove it
- A pair in which neither record shows the room door opening or the item leaving cannot prove the way out.
- A booking for the opening is fine for the staff member and says nothing about a visitor who slipped in behind.
- A visitor log with only sign-in and sign-out shows presence in the building, not at the door. Use it for the exit path, not the pair.
- A routine main-exit badge-out plus anything else does not show the room entry was unbooked.
- A booked fetch followed by a pickup is normal on its own; neither shows invalid authorisation.
- A pickup row plus a record not showing the quoted ticket's status or item cannot prove invalid release.
- A request quoting a bad reference shows intent, not collection. A booking plus a closed ticket without the pickup row shows no carrier.
- Motive records point you somewhere but prove neither presence nor release.

## Budget discipline
- Full solves have taken about twenty to thirty commands. Plan for thirty and keep several in reserve for filing.
- Filter logs by room, person, reference, and time window instead of paging through everything.
- When an entry has no matching booking, check the person's calendar and messages for that minute.
- When an entry is booked, check tours, escorts, and corridor notes for that minute before clearing it; this is often the fastest route to the answer.
- When a fetch looks legitimate, open the request, every reference it quotes (governing one first), then the carrier row, and check whether the claimed requester actually asked.
- Stop on someone once a legitimate reason fully explains them, unless it rests on an unverified request.
- File only when each flag rests on an explicit entry you have seen and the person, way out, and exit path agree.

## General cautions
- Never assume a staff member, visitor, outsider, or any particular way out is always the answer. Let the records decide.
- An outside request is only as good as the reference it quotes; verify its status, item, and position in the quote every time.
