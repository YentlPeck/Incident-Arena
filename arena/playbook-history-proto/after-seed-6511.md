# Locked-Room Prototype Playbook

## Goal and framing
- Every case asks who carried the prototype out, how it left the locked room and the building, and which two records together prove it.
- Read the house rules first. They define what counts as proof, how records are filed, and quirks such as which reference on a request governs it.
- A correct filing needs the right person, the right way out, and a pair the game accepts. A right guess with weak evidence still fails.
- Cases have accepted one, two, or three pairs. Test each candidate pair against what each record literally shows, not what it implies.

## Opening moves
- List every record type: door or badge logs, bookings, calendars, chats, visitor logs, tour or escort schedules, corridor notes, inventory checks, exit or bag-check logs, pickup requests, carrier logs, work tickets.
- Fix the window between the prototype's last confirmed presence and first noticed absence, then pull the locked-room door log for it. Each opening is a candidate moment.

## Linking records
- For each opening, ask who opened it, why (booking or task), and who else was near at that minute.
- A legitimate booking explains the opening, not the outcome. It clears the opener's entry, never whoever followed them in, and never a task resting on a bad request.
- Match bookings to the exact minute. A booking by the same person at another time does not cover an entry outside that slot.
- A calendar slot or the person's own chat placing them elsewhere at an entry's minute is strong evidence of an unjustified entry.
- People without badge access (visitors, tours, contractors, delivery staff) can only enter through someone else's opening. Check tour routes, escort schedules, and corridor notes for a group passing the room at that minute, then tie each visitor to their group via the visitor log or escort list.
- A record placing someone at the doorway at the exact minute it opened is the strongest link.
- Follow the item to the building exit: badge-out, sign-out, end of escort, or a carrier handover. Match handover time to fetch time.

## Ways out and the pairs that prove them
- General rule: the door-log row (or the item-leaving row) plus one independent record showing that event was illegitimate forms a pair. Count independent contradicting records to predict how many pairs exist.
- Tailgating: the door-log row for the booked opening plus a record putting the visitor's group at that doorway then (tour route, escort schedule, corridor note). Each independent placement record completes its own pair; two such records gave two accepted pairs.
- Misused badge: an entry in the holder's name plus a record showing the holder was elsewhere.
- Insider without reason: their own entry plus any record showing nothing covers it. Calendar elsewhere, their own chat saying where they were, and their only booking being at another time have each completed a separate pair with the entry row.
- Carried out by a person: presence at the room plus a later exit record; bag-check gaps strengthen it. The exit row supports the story but is rarely half of the proving pair.
- Fraudulent outside pickup: an innocent booked staff fetch handed to a carrier, triggered by a request posing as a known partner and quoting a reference that does not authorise release. The accepted pair has been the carrier pickup row plus the ticket showing that reference is closed, old, or not for the prototype.
- A pickup carries the item of the reference quoted first. A reference quoted only "also" is a decoy even when open.
- Check the claimed partner's own records: if they never asked, the request is forged.
- Before filing: one record must show the room entry or item leaving, the other why it was illegitimate, tied to the person or carrier.

## Tempting pairs that do not prove it
- A pair in which neither record shows the room door opening or the item leaving cannot prove the way out; calendar plus chat is such a pair.
- A booking for the opening clears the staff member and says nothing about a visitor who slipped in behind.
- A visitor log with only sign-in and sign-out shows presence in the building, not at the door. Use it to name the visitor and the exit, never as half of a tailgating pair.
- A routine main-exit badge-out plus anything else does not show the room entry was unbooked; everyone badges out.
- A booked fetch plus a pickup is normal; neither shows invalid authorisation. A request quoting a bad reference shows intent, not collection.
- A pickup row plus a record not showing the quoted ticket's status and item, or plus the "also" ticket, proves nothing about release.
- Motive records point you somewhere but prove neither presence nor release.

## Budget discipline
- Full solves have taken about twenty to thirty commands. Plan for thirty and keep several in reserve for filing.
- Filter logs by room, person, reference, and time window instead of paging through everything.
- Entry with no matching booking: check that person's bookings for the whole day, their calendar, and their messages for that minute.
- Entry that is booked: before clearing it, pull tours, escorts, and corridor notes for that exact minute; a group passing then usually decides the case, and the visitor log then names who left and when.
- Booked fetch followed by a carrier handover: go straight to the request, its governing reference, then the carrier row.
- Stop on someone once a legitimate reason fully explains them, unless it rests on an unverified request.
- File only when each flag rests on an explicit entry you have seen and the person, way out, and exit path agree.

## General cautions
- Never assume a staff member, visitor, outsider, or any particular way out is always the answer. Let the records decide.
- An outside request is only as good as the reference it quotes; verify its status, item, and position in the quote every time.
