# Locked-Room Prototype Playbook

## Goal and framing
- Every case asks who carried the prototype out, how it left the locked room and the building, and which two records together prove it.
- Read the house rules first. They define what counts as proof, how records are filed, and quirks such as which reference on a request actually governs it.
- A correct filing needs the right person, the right way out, and an evidence pair the game accepts. A right guess with weak evidence still fails.
- Often only one pair is accepted, so test each candidate pair against what each record literally shows.

## Opening moves
- List every record type before opening any single record: door or badge logs, room bookings, visitor logs, tour or escort schedules, corridor notes, inventory checks, exit or bag-check logs, outside pickup requests, carrier or dispatch logs, repair or work tickets.
- Fix the window between the prototype's last confirmed presence and first noticed absence.
- Pull the locked-room door log filtered to that window. Each opening is a candidate moment.

## Linking records
- For each opening, ask who opened it, why (booking or stated task), and who else was near at that moment.
- A legitimate booking explains the opening, not the outcome. A staff member can fetch the item in good faith for a request that was itself invalid.
- Check people with no badge access: visitors, tours, contractors, delivery staff. They can only enter through someone else's opening.
- Cross-reference place and time together. A record placing someone at the doorway at the exact minute it opened is the strongest link.
- Follow the item to the building exit: sign-out, exit scan, end of escort, or a handover to an outside carrier.

## Ways out and the pairs that prove them
- Tailgating: the door log entry for that opening plus a record putting the suspect at that doorway then.
- Misused badge: an entry in the holder's name plus a record showing the holder was elsewhere.
- Insider without reason: their own entry in the window plus a record showing no booking or task.
- Carried out by a person: presence at the room plus a later exit record; bag-check gaps strengthen it.
- Fraudulent outside pickup: a legitimate fetch and handover to a carrier, triggered by a request quoting a reference that does not authorise release. Prove it by pairing the carrier log pickup row (the record of actual collection, and of which reference it quotes) with the ticket or work record showing that reference is closed, old, or not for the prototype.
- When a request or pickup quotes several references, check which one governs it (often the first quoted). A reference quoted only alongside it does not decide what was released.
- Confirm the pair before filing: one record must show the item actually leaving or the door opening, the other must show why that was illegitimate and tie it to the person or carrier.

## Tempting pairs that do not prove it
- Two records that never show the item leaving or the door opening cannot prove the way out.
- A booked fetch followed by a pickup is normal on its own; neither shows the authorisation was invalid.
- A request quoting a bad reference shows intent, not collection. Only the carrier pickup row shows something was collected.
- A booking plus a closed ticket, without the pickup row, does not show which carrier took anything.
- A visitor log with only sign-in and sign-out shows presence in the building, not at the door.
- Motive records point you somewhere but do not prove presence or release.

## Budget discipline
- Full solves have taken roughly two dozen to thirty commands. Plan for about thirty and keep a reserve for filing.
- Filter logs by room, reference, and time window instead of paging through everything.
- When a fetch looks legitimate, spend the next command on what authorised it: open the request, then the reference it quotes, then the carrier log.
- Stop investigating someone once a legitimate reason fully explains them, unless the reason itself rests on an unverified request.
- Before filing, check each flag is backed by an explicit entry you have seen, not by inference.
- File only when both records are identified and the person, way out, and exit path agree.

## General cautions
- Never assume a staff member, visitor, outsider, or any particular way out is always the answer. Let the records decide.
- An outside request is only as good as the reference it quotes; verify that reference's status and item every time.
