# Locked-Room Prototype Playbook

## Goal and framing
- Every case asks three things: who carried the prototype out, how it got out of the locked room and the building, and which two records together prove it.
- Read the house rules shown in the game first. They define what counts as proof, how records are filed, and what a filing costs.
- A correct answer needs the right person, the right way out, and an evidence pair the game accepts. A right guess filed with weak evidence still fails.
- More than one evidence pair can be accepted. You need one solid pair, not every possible one.

## Opening moves (spend the first few commands here)
- List every record type before opening any single record: door or badge logs, room bookings, visitor sign-in logs, tour or escort schedules, corridor or camera notes, inventory checks, exit or bag-check logs.
- Find when the prototype was last confirmed present and when it was first found missing. Everything after that is built on this window.
- Pull the door log for the locked room and limit it to that window. Each door opening is a candidate moment for the theft.

## Linking records
- For each door opening, ask who opened it, why (a booking or stated task), and who else was physically near at that moment.
- A legitimate reason, such as a booked test, clears the badge holder for that opening. It does not clear anyone standing behind them.
- Check people with no badge access of their own: visitors, tour groups, contractors, delivery staff. They can only get in through someone else's opening.
- Cross-reference by place and time together. A record placing a person at the corridor or doorway of the locked room at the exact minute it opened is the strongest link.
- Tour routes and escort schedules often list which corridors were passed and when. Match those times against door openings minute by minute.
- After placing someone at the room, follow them to the building exit: sign-out, exit scan, or end of escort. A sign-out soon after the opening fits carrying the item out.

## Ways out and the pairs that prove them
- Tailgating behind an authorised opener: pair the door log entry for that opening with a record putting the suspect at that doorway at that moment, such as a tour route, escort schedule, or corridor sighting.
- Misused or borrowed badge: pair a badge entry in the holder's name with a record showing the holder was elsewhere at that time.
- Insider with access: pair their own door entry inside the window with a record showing no legitimate booking or task.
- Carried out of the building: pair presence at the room with a later exit record. Bag-check or exit-scan gaps strengthen this.
- Confirm the pair before filing: one record must show the door actually opening, and the other must tie the suspect to that opening.

## Tempting pairs that do not prove it
- Two records that never show the locked-room door opening cannot prove entry, however suspicious they look.
- A booking that explains why the door opened speaks only to the staff member, not to others present.
- A visitor log with only sign-in and sign-out times shows the visitor was in the building, not where they were when the door opened.
- Motive records, such as rival employers or complaints, only point you somewhere. They are not proof of presence.
- Being in the building during the window is not the same as being at the door when it opened.

## Budget discipline
- Full solves have taken roughly two dozen to thirty commands. Plan for about thirty and keep a reserve for filing.
- Filter logs by room and time window instead of paging through everything.
- Once a door opening has a legitimate opener, spend the next command on who was near it, not on re-checking the opener.
- Stop investigating a suspect once a legitimate reason fully explains their presence. Move on to whoever else was there.
- Before filing, check each flag or record is backed by an explicit entry you have seen, not by inference alone.
- File only when both records are identified and the person, way out, and exit path all agree.

## General cautions
- Never assume a staff member, a visitor, or any particular way out is always the answer. Let the door openings and the records placing people at them decide.
- Re-read any hints in the house rules for how the game words acceptable evidence.
