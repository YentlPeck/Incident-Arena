# Locked-Room Prototype Playbook

## Goal and framing
- Every case asks who carried the prototype out, how it left the locked room and the building, and which two records together prove it.
- Read the house rules first. They define what counts as proof, how records are filed, and quirks such as which reference on a request governs it.
- A correct filing needs the right person, the right way out, and an evidence pair the game accepts. A right guess with weak evidence still fails.
- Some cases accept one pair and others accept several. Test each candidate pair against what each record literally shows, not what it implies.

## Opening moves
- List every record type before opening any single record: door or badge logs, room bookings, calendars, chat messages, visitor logs, escort schedules, corridor notes, inventory checks, exit or bag-check logs, pickup requests, carrier logs, work tickets.
- Fix the window between the prototype's last confirmed presence and first noticed absence.
- Pull the locked-room door log for that window. Each opening is a candidate moment.

## Linking records
- For each opening, ask who opened it, why (booking or stated task), and who else was near at that moment.
- A legitimate booking explains the opening, not the outcome. A staff member can fetch in good faith, in a booked slot, for a request that was itself invalid.
- Match bookings to the exact minute. A booking by the same person at another time of day does not cover an entry outside that slot.
- Check alibis against entries. A calendar slot or the person's own chat message placing them elsewhere at the minute of an entry is strong evidence of an unjustified entry.
- Check people with no badge access: visitors, tours, contractors, delivery staff. They can only enter through someone else's opening.
- Cross-reference place and time together. A record placing someone at the doorway at the exact minute it opened is the strongest link.
- Follow the item to the building exit: badge-out, sign-out, end of escort, or a handover to a carrier. Match the handover time to the fetch time.

## Ways out and the pairs that prove them
- Tailgating: the door log entry for that opening plus a record putting the suspect at that doorway then.
- Misused badge: an entry in the holder's name plus a record showing the holder was elsewhere.
- Insider without reason: their own room entry in the window plus a record showing no booking or task covers it, such as a calendar or chat placing them elsewhere, or their only booking at a different time. Several such records can each complete a valid pair.
- Carried out by a person: presence at the room plus a later exit record; bag-check gaps strengthen it.
- Fraudulent outside pickup: a legitimate fetch handed to a carrier, triggered by a request posing as a known partner and quoting a reference that does not authorise release. Pair the carrier log pickup row (actual collection and the reference it quotes) with the ticket showing that reference is closed, old, or not for the prototype.
- When a request quotes several references, check which one governs it (the first quoted has governed so far). A reference quoted only "also" does not decide what was released, even if open; it is often a decoy.
- Confirm the pair before filing: one record must show the room entry or the item leaving, the other must show why that was illegitimate and tie it to the person or carrier.

## Tempting pairs that do not prove it
- A pair in which neither record shows the room entry or the item leaving cannot prove the way out.
- A routine building exit, such as a main-exit badge-out every staff member makes, plus anything else does not show the room entry was unbooked.
- A booked fetch followed by a pickup is normal on its own; neither shows the authorisation was invalid.
- A pickup row plus a record not showing the quoted ticket's status or item cannot prove invalid release.
- A request quoting a bad reference shows intent, not collection. Only the carrier pickup row shows collection.
- A booking plus a closed ticket, without the pickup row, does not show which carrier took anything.
- A pairing built on the secondary "also" reference proves nothing about what was released.
- A visitor log with only sign-in and sign-out shows presence in the building, not at the door.
- Motive records point you somewhere but do not prove presence or release.

## Budget discipline
- Full solves have taken about two dozen to thirty commands. Plan for thirty and keep several in reserve for filing.
- Filter logs by room, person, reference, and time window instead of paging through everything.
- When an entry has no matching booking, spend the next commands on the person's calendar and messages for that minute.
- When a fetch looks legitimate, open the request, then every reference it quotes (governing one first), then the carrier log row.
- Check whether the claimed requester actually asked; a request posing as a known partner is a strong lead.
- Stop on someone once a legitimate reason fully explains them, unless that reason rests on an unverified request.
- Before filing, check each flag is backed by an explicit entry you have seen, not by inference.
- File only when both records are identified and the person, way out, and exit path agree.

## General cautions
- Never assume a staff member, visitor, outsider, or any particular way out is always the answer. Let the records decide.
- An outside request is only as good as the reference it quotes; verify that reference's status, item, and position in the quote every time.
