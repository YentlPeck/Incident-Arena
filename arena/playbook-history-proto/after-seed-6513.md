# Locked-Room Prototype Playbook

## Goal and framing
- Every case asks who carried the prototype out, how it left the room and the building, and which two records together prove it.
- Read the house rules first. They define proof, filing format, and quirks such as which reference on a request governs it.
- A correct filing needs the right person, the right way out, and a pair the game accepts. A right guess on weak evidence still fails.
- Cases have accepted one, two, or three pairs. Test each pair against what each record literally shows, not what it implies.

## Opening moves
- List every record type: door or badge logs, bookings, calendars, chats, visitor logs, tours or escorts, corridor notes, inventory checks, exit or bag-check logs, pickup requests, carrier logs, work tickets.
- Fix the window between last confirmed presence and first noticed absence, then pull the locked-room door log for it. Each opening is a candidate moment.
- If a carrier log or pickup request exists, check early whether any pickup follows an opening; that branch resolves fast.

## Linking records
- For each opening, ask who opened it, why (booking or task), and who else was near at that minute.
- A legitimate booking explains the opening, not the outcome. It clears the opener's entry, never whoever followed them in, and never a task resting on a bad request.
- Match bookings to the exact minute. A booking by the same person at another time does not cover an entry outside that slot.
- A calendar slot or the person's own chat placing them elsewhere at an entry's minute is strong evidence of an unjustified entry.
- People without badge access can only enter through someone else's opening. Check tours, escorts, and corridor notes for a group at the room then, and tie each visitor to their group.
- Follow the item to the building exit: badge-out, sign-out, end of escort, or a carrier handover. Match handover time to fetch time.

## Ways out and the pairs that prove them
- General rule: the door-log row (or item-leaving row) plus one independent record showing that event was illegitimate. Count independent contradicting records to predict how many pairs exist.
- Tailgating: the door-log row for the booked opening plus a record putting the visitor's group at that doorway then. Each independent placement record completes its own pair.
- Misused badge: an entry in the holder's name plus a record showing the holder was elsewhere.
- Insider without reason: their own entry plus any record showing nothing covers it; calendar elsewhere, own chat, or only booking at another time have each completed a separate pair.
- Carried out by a person: presence at the room plus a later exit record; bag-check gaps strengthen it. The exit row is rarely half of the proving pair.
- Fraudulent outside pickup (the most frequent pattern so far, but never assume it): an innocent booked staff fetch handed to a carrier, triggered by a request posing as a known partner and quoting a reference that does not authorise release. The only accepted pair has been the carrier pickup row plus the ticket showing the governing reference is closed or old (even when it is for the prototype itself) or for a different item. Expect exactly one pair.
- A pickup carries the item of the reference quoted first. A reference quoted only "also" is a decoy even when open and for another item.
- Check the claimed partner's own records: if they never asked, the request is forged. The carrier, not the fetching staff member, carried it out of the building.

## Tempting pairs that do not prove it
- A pair in which neither record shows the room door opening or the item leaving cannot prove the way out; calendar plus chat is such a pair.
- A booking for the opening clears the staff member and says nothing about a visitor who slipped in behind.
- A visitor log with only sign-in and sign-out shows presence in the building, not at the door.
- A routine main-exit badge-out plus anything else does not show the room entry was unbooked.
- A booked fetch plus a pickup is normal; neither shows the authorising ticket is invalid.
- A booked fetch plus the closed ticket does not show which carrier took anything out.
- The request plus the closed ticket shows intent, not collection; the carrier pickup row is the record of collection.
- A pickup row plus a record not showing the quoted ticket's status and item, or plus the "also" ticket, proves nothing about release.
- Motive records point you somewhere but prove neither presence nor release.

## Budget discipline
- Full solves have taken about twenty to thirty-two commands; pickup cases cluster near the middle. Plan for thirty-five and keep several in reserve for filing.
- Filter logs by room, person, reference, and time window instead of paging; never open a record twice.
- Unbooked entry: check that person's bookings for the day, their calendar, and their messages for that minute.
- Booked entry: pull tours, escorts, and corridor notes for that exact minute before clearing it.
- Booked fetch then carrier handover: go straight to the request, its first-quoted reference, that ticket, the carrier row, then the partner's own records.
- Stop on someone once a legitimate reason fully explains them, unless it rests on an unverified request.
- File only when each flag rests on an explicit entry you have seen and the person, way out, and exit path agree.

## General cautions
- Never assume a staff member, visitor, outsider, or any way out is always the answer. Let the records decide.
- An outside request is only as good as the reference it quotes; verify status, item, and position in the quote every time.
